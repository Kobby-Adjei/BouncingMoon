# BouncingMoon Problem Brief

## Who We Are Solving For

BouncingMoon is built for people who know what they want to do on a computer, but cannot reliably perform the fine motor actions required to do it.

Our primary users are people with Parkinson's disease, especially those affected by tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, or freezing that make cursor control, typing, and tapping difficult.

Secondary users include:

- People with essential tremor
- People with cerebral palsy
- People with multiple sclerosis
- Stroke survivors with limited hand control
- People with arthritis or other dexterity-limiting conditions
- People with temporary motor impairments

## The Problem

Many users with motor-control impairments can still make decisions, communicate intent, and understand digital workflows. The barrier is not cognition. The barrier is execution.

Modern computers assume precise physical input:

- Move the cursor accurately
- Click a small target
- Type quickly and cleanly
- Navigate menus without shaking or missing

For many users, these actions are exhausting, slow, frustrating, or impossible.

Existing accessibility tools often help with dictation or basic voice control, but they do not reliably complete multi-step desktop tasks on the user's behalf.

## What BouncingMoon Does

BouncingMoon helps people with motor-control impairments operate a computer through accessible input and agent-driven assistance instead of precise manual control.

The system lets users express intent through voice and rhythmic input, then uses a background agent to handle the precision work:

- Clicking the right button
- Filling forms
- Typing messages
- Opening apps
- Navigating interfaces
- Reading the screen and asking for confirmation before risky actions

## Product Framing

BouncingMoon is not just a thump detector.

It is an assistive operating layer that translates human intent into safe system actions.

Thumps are one input modality. Voice is another. The real product is a background accessibility agent that helps users complete digital tasks they would otherwise struggle to perform independently.

## One-Line Definition

BouncingMoon helps people with motor-control impairments operate their computer through voice, rhythm, and agent-driven assistance instead of precise manual input.

## Short Pitch

BouncingMoon is an accessibility assistant for people with Parkinson's and other motor impairments. Instead of forcing users to control a computer through precise clicks and typing, it lets them express intent through voice and simple physical signals while an agent completes the task safely in the background.
